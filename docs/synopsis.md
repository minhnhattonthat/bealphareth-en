# Synopsis (spoilers)

Sources: ja.wikipedia "ベアルファレス", wikiwiki.jp/yukashi (fan guide), a read of `script/`. The extracted script decides wording. Do not give late reveals away in early lines.

## World
A thousand years of peace have rotted the kings, nobles and clergy, and commoners are crushed by tax. Twelve nations share the continent; the kingdom of Asroit and the republic of Baileste contend for it. An earthquake on their border opens the Kars mountains, where the **Azare Stone**, said to grant eternal life, is found, and beneath it the ruins of the legendary **Sun Empire**: its capital **Asla Fael**. Monsters burst out and destroy both armies. The ruin is walled off and sealed; later, short of money, both countries reopen it to commoners and foreigners.

Inside the walls grows **Kars Bastide**, an adventurers' town its people call the **Coffin of Kars**. No law, army or church reaches it; the only rule is strength. Inside the ruin common sense fails, and people grow far stronger than anyone outside. Calendar: Anoian year 5098 at the start. Faith: the Anoian Church, founded by the prophet Anoia and the Thirteen Saints, dualist (Zemen and Zeweak, the Two Supreme Gods).

Nations: Asroit (largest, corrupt, four dukes who ignore the throne), Baileste (coastal, wealthy, oligarchs rule its democracy), Lenos (papal state), Tradoa (poor, naive, witch trials), Fisano (city league, declining), Biastok (cold far north), Krasdar (religious, outside the Pope's reach), Fakolz (young duchy, split from Asroit), Racoss (old, Baileste's vassal), Luminia (ancient, trade cities in the east), Irenia (half an island, Baileste's vassal), Sefita (tribal union, exploited by Baileste).

## Story by chapter
1. **Black Passage.** The hero (default Weld or Filia; player sets name, gender, class, status and purpose) and 14 young newcomers arrive and meet Eugen, Valdes and Qumran. Early jobs: the touchstone test, the Droplet Stone, Kadmon's shabby blackmail over a medicine for Ash's sister, the Rahmel leaf in the Blue Passage, a rescue.
2. **Asla Fael.** The party seeks the **Sun Jewel** in the First Temple (Temple of the Sun). The hero and two companions are taken over by the **Berserker's Sword** (the Purple Sword) and massacre people in town. Valdes stops them at great cost to himself; the hero is left comatose for months, and the town hates them ("that berserker bastard").
3. **Purgatory.** Qumran finds the berserkers' souls are bound in the **Pillars of Sheol**; destroying the pillars saves them. Whether to break the last pillar is the hero's call.
4. **Corridor of Time.** The ruin is a spirit world: monsters come from the spirit and material worlds fusing. Valdes gives his life to open the door. His greatsword passes to the hero.
5. **Celestial Realm.** Angels attack; the Red Lion Knights slaughter townsfolk; the Thirteen Saints tempt the hero; Luca turns against the party.
6. **Sea of Spirit.** Only the hero and a chosen partner enter. Everyone's mind takes form here, with Phantoms of each companion (虚像).
7. **Land of Origin.** Battles with the Saints. Avadail, their leader, tells the truth: the Sun Empire's real name is **Bealphareth**, and the collective mind of its people became a god working toward the **New World**, with no time, space, sickness, death or self. The hero is a soulless "puppet", cast as the Devil the prophecy needs. They kill the god.

Epilogue: the New World is stopped, but the two great powers are gone and the world falls into the Age of Twilight. The ruin collapses and the town empties. The hero and partner live on. There is always a partner, no solo ending.

## Late reveals, keep hidden early
- The Azare Stone gives no eternal life by itself: it gives a body whose soul was cut out by the Berserker's Sword a false personality and deathless strength.
- The Thirteen Saints are thought-forms of the Sun Empire who ride human souls. Barkail serves absolutely; Avadail wrote the Church's rules, and in the end entrusts the god's fall to the hero. Hanrial rides Nada, Welkail rides Fatum: together they wrote the Scriptures.
- The hero is a false being; "[HERO]'s Phantom" scenes and "soulless puppet" lines refer to this.
- Fatum and Nada: siblings experimented on, made undying by a noble. Sadiya knows their past.
- Valdes's death, Qumran's death, Luca's turn against the party.

## People
Companions (17-19): **Ash** farmer boy, axe, kind and homely, saves money for his sick sister and carves wooden dolls. **Arthur** earnest young knight dreaming of heroes. **Leia** daughter of a fallen knight, stiff and proud, out to restore her house. **Eleanor** noble lady in flight, who killed her father by accident. **Pasca** rough hunter, working to save his village. **Scharn** cheerful martial artist, seeking her brother. **Jessica** sharp-tongued thief with a code. **Orpheus** self-styled noble, polite, sarcastic skirt-chaser. **Eve** cool bounty hunter here "because it looks fun". **Dias** taciturn, cold, origin unknown. **Noel** prickly scholar girl, a knight's daughter, here to see Qumran. **Luca** timid stammering monk. **Sara** sweet orphanage girl earning for the orphans.

Town: **Valdes** (29) strongest fighter, warm-hearted rough big brother. **Eugen** (58) tavern keeper, gruff old ex-captain. **Qumran** (31) gentle scholar, foremost on the ruins. **Tiara** (17) young priest, healer, gentle. **Kadmon** (38) crooked, greedy deacon who calls himself "the great Kadmon". **Sadiya** (22) Sedik poisoner, sells medicine. **Fatum** undying boy, looks 15. **Nada** his sister, heart gone. **Gaius** (34) grim swordsmith who enjoys killing.

Saints: **Avadail**, serene, addresses the hero as "you" like a teacher. **Barkail**, old, archaic. **Welkail**, **Hanrial**.
